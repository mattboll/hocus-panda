# Hocuspanda 1.0.0

The first release of Hocuspanda: the desktop of a developer who works with
AI agents. They see at a glance what their agents are doing, and
everything else is beautiful, fluid and never in the way
([vision](../vision.md)).

Hocuspanda is a Wayland compositor on smithay, a shell (the bar, its
popovers, the launcher, notifications, the lock screen's interface) drawn
with iced as a separate process that the compositor starts and restarts,
and a Settings application. A crash or a restart of the shell keeps every
window in place. It is for developers running several agents on one or
more screens, from 1366×768 up; it is not aimed at the general public yet.

Everything the desktop says is in English and French.

## Install

Download `hocuspanda_1.0.0-1_amd64.deb` from the release, then:

```sh
sudo apt install ./hocuspanda_1.0.0-1_amd64.deb
```

Log out: the display manager (GDM, SDDM, greetd's greeters) offers the
"Hocuspanda" session. `Super+Return` opens a terminal, `Super+Space` the
launcher, `Super+P` every action, `Super+Shift+E` logs out. If the session
does not start, the display manager comes back and the reason is in
`journalctl --user -t hocuspanda -b`. What the package installs:
[packaging/README.md](../../packaging/README.md).

## Requirements

- Debian testing on amd64. The package's library dependencies are those
  of the system it was built on, so it installs on that release and later
  ones.
- Depends: the libraries found at build time (libinput, libseat, libgbm,
  libxkbcommon, libpixman, libpipewire among them), `libegl1`,
  `libpam0g`, `libpam-modules`, `xdg-desktop-portal`.
- Recommends, each one only hiding its feature when missing: Mesa's
  drivers, `xwayland`, `dbus-user-session`, a terminal (kitty, foot,
  alacritty or xterm), UPower, power-profiles-daemon (or tuned-ppd),
  PipeWire and WirePlumber, NetworkManager, BlueZ, `xdg-utils`, a colour
  emoji font, `xdg-desktop-portal-gtk`, `ffmpeg` and a VA-API driver,
  GNOME Online Accounts, polkit.
- A GPU is optional. The compositor draws with GLES and falls back to
  pixman on the CPU when GLES does not start; the shell draws with Vulkan
  and falls back to tiny-skia. On the CPU there is no blur, fades replace
  some animations, the halo becomes a tint of the agents' rows and the
  pandas' moods do not play.

## What is in 1.0

### The bar

A column on the right of the screen (or the left), in two forms: full and
mini, chosen per screen (`Super+Shift+B`, or a click on its outer edge).
Windows may cover it; pushing the pointer against the edge brings it in
front. `Super+B` takes the keyboard into it (Tab, arrows, Enter, Esc).

- **Clock and date**, whose digits roll on each minute; its font, colour,
  seconds and transition are set in `[panel.clock]`. A click opens the
  calendar.
- **Levels**: volume, brightness and battery, the media keys, and their
  popovers: outputs, the microphone and the volume of each application;
  the screen and keyboard backlight; the battery's state, health and
  power mode.
- **Connections**: Wi-Fi (join open, WPA2 and WPA3 networks, paste a
  password), Bluetooth (connect, pair, devices' battery), the keyboard
  layout.
- **Desktops**: tiles sketching each desktop's windows with their icons;
  hover lists them, a click switches, the wheel steps through them, a
  window dropped on a tile goes to that desktop. The current desktop's
  windows are listed above them, with their menu.
- **In use**: the microphone, the camera, a screen share or a recording,
  with what stops them.
- **Tray**: applications' StatusNotifierItem icons and menus.
- **Footer**: the clipboard's history (`Super+V`: search, pin, paste into
  the previous window; kept encrypted, passwords skipped), the
  notifications' bell, the launcher, and the session menu (Settings, Lock,
  Suspend, Log out, Restart, Shut down, with a countdown that names what
  holds them back).
- **Launcher** (`Super+Space`, or four fingers up): applications by fuzzy
  search, calculations, unit conversions, addresses, web search, emoji.
- **Emoji picker** (`Super+period`), at the text caret, with skin tones and
  recent emoji.
- Every group can be hidden or reordered, live, from the Settings or
  `[panel]`.

### Your day

- Google calendars through GNOME Online Accounts (no sign-in of its own):
  which calendars show, their colours, which are featured.
- Tasks from a Planka board (by a pasted access token) or a local list.
  Set up from the bar in three steps; everything is cached for offline
  use.
- The next meeting's card, with a Join button when it has a video link;
  the rest of today's events; three tasks to tick or add.
- A month calendar with the day's agenda.
- Reminders sliding out of the bar at the event's alarm (Join, Later,
  dismiss), the halo before a meeting, and `Super+J` to join the current
  or next call.

### Agents and the pandas

- Claude Code and Codex followed through their hooks, plugged in from the
  bar after asking (the original settings are backed up, and restored
  when unplugged); herdr followed while it runs; any other tool with
  `hocus agent NAME STATE` ([docs/agents.md](../agents.md)).
- One row per agent, with its panda: working, waiting for you, stuck, done
  and not seen yet, resting. An agent that waits or failed shows what it
  asks or why. A click brings its window, with a streak from the row.
- The secretary panda heads the group and briefs on who waits.
- The halo lights the whole bar when an agent waits (or a meeting is
  near); a reminder comes after a while; a panda peeks out of the bar when
  an agent starts waiting or finishes. Each is set on the Agents page.
- In the overview, windows carry their agent's panda.

### Notifications

- A notification server: bubbles at the top right with icons, actions and
  progress, at most three at once; urgent ones stay.
- A history from the bell, grouped by time, kept seven days.
- Do not disturb for an hour, until 8:00 (the real clock time, across a
  suspend too) or until turned off.
- Held while the screen is shared, then summed up in one bubble.

### Screen sharing, recording and captures

- The desktop's own ScreenCast portal over PipeWire: a chooser for screens
  and windows with live thumbnails, a ring around what is shared (not in
  the stream), and a sign in the bar to stop it.
- `Print` captures an area, a window, a screen or all screens, now or
  after a delay; `Shift+Print` and `Alt+Print` take the screen or the
  window at once. Captures go to the clipboard and to `Pictures`; a
  thumbnail opens an editor (arrows, boxes, text, a real blur).
- Recordings of an area, a window or a screen, with the sound and the
  microphone, encoded by ffmpeg (on the GPU with VA-API when there is
  one); `Super+Shift+R` starts and stops.
- Drawing on the screen: `Super`+drag draws a stroke, `Super+Shift`+drag an
  arrow, `Super+Alt` shows a laser pointer; strokes fade away.

### Windows and desktops

- Floating windows that snap to halves, quarters and the whole screen by
  dragging or with `Super`+arrows; magnetic edges; two windows side by
  side resize together; wobbly windows while they move.
- A grip on windows that leave their decorations to the desktop (Wayland
  and X11): drag to move, double-click to maximise, right-click for the
  radial menu.
- The radial menu (`Super` + right click): maximise, always on top, on all
  desktops, next or previous desktop, other screen, minimise, close.
- `Super+P`: every action of the desktop, searchable, with the recent ones.
- The overview (`Super+W`, four fingers down, or the top-left corner):
  every screen's windows and desktops at once, to drag windows between
  them.
- Desktops per screen (four by default, up to nine, with names):
  `Super+1…9`, `Super+Ctrl+←/→`, three fingers sideways; send or take a
  window along with `Super+Shift` and `Super+Ctrl+Shift`.
- Alt+Tab in most-recently-used order with live thumbnails.
- Rules per application: its desktop, its size, above the others.
- Window opacity with `Super+Alt`+wheel.
- X11 applications through Xwayland.

### Several screens

- Screens of different scales side by side: a window keeps its logical
  size on each and draws at the largest scale it overlaps.
- Screens recognised by their identity; each set of screens keeps its own
  arrangement. Unplugging a screen moves its windows to another; plugging
  it back returns them. Closing the lid with another screen counts as
  unplugging.
- `Super+Alt+←/→` moves a window to the next screen.
- Night light on a schedule.

### Lock, idle and session

- The lock screen (`Super+L`, the session menu, idle, or before sleep):
  the password checked by PAM, the wallpaper, a clock, the waiting
  agents, sleeping pandas. Other lockers using ext-session-lock work too.
- Idle: the screen dims, locks, then turns off, with different delays on
  battery; applications and full-screen video hold it off
  (`org.freedesktop.ScreenSaver`, idle inhibitors).
- Suspend after a delay, the lid and the power button, each configurable.
- The session from a display manager; the start-up applications of XDG
  autostart; the last session's applications reopened on their screen,
  desktop and place at login.
- The system's authentication dialog: the shell is the session's polkit
  agent.

### Settings

One window (`hocus-settings`, from the launcher or the session menu) whose
every change applies at once and can be undone (`Ctrl+Z`). It searches
with `Ctrl+F`, keeps comments in `config.toml` when it writes it, and
offers the last version that read when the file is broken. Its pages:

- **Power**: the battery, delays to dim, turn off and suspend on battery
  and on AC, the lid and the power button, the power mode.
- **Sound**: outputs, inputs, profiles, balance, each application's
  volume and output.
- **Bluetooth**: devices paired and around, pairing with a code, rename,
  forget.
- **Network**: Wi-Fi, saved, hidden and enterprise networks, airplane
  mode, wired, VPNs imported from WireGuard and OpenVPN files.
- **Displays**: arrangement, resolution and refresh rate (kept only once
  confirmed), scale, variable refresh rate, the primary screen, screen
  sets, night light.
- **Mouse and touchpad**: speed, tapping, scrolling, and the touchpad's
  gestures.
- **Keyboard and shortcuts**: layouts, repeat, options, and every
  shortcut.
- **Appearance and language**: dark or light, the accent colour, text and
  cursor sizes, English or French, date and time formats.
- **Accessibility**: see below.
- **Wallpaper**: built-in backgrounds, a colour or a picture, per screen,
  and the lock screen's.
- **Bar**: its side, its screens, its form, its groups and their order,
  the clock.
- **Account**: the avatar (a panda or a picture), the display name, the
  password.
- **Date and time**: automatic time, the time by hand, the time zone.
- **Desktops and windows**: desktops, moving and snapping, wobbly
  windows, Alt+Tab, the hot corner, drawing on the screen, rules per
  application.
- **Agents**: the tools followed, the halo, the reminder, the peek, the
  pandas.
- **Applications**: default applications, the terminal, the start-up
  applications, reopening the last session.
- **Screenshots and clipboard**: folders and formats, the shutter sound,
  the clipboard's history.

### Accessibility

- A screen reader: the bar, its popovers, the notifications, the lock
  screen and the authentication dialog described on AT-SPI; Orca started
  with the session or with `Super+Alt+S`; agents that start waiting are
  announced.
- The whole bar from the keyboard (`Super+B`).
- High contrast (told to applications through the portal), reduced
  transparency and reduced motion.
- A magnifier up to 16× (`Super+=`, `Super+-`, `Super+Esc`, `Super`+wheel)
  that follows the pointer, the focus and the text caret, on the lock
  screen too, and is left out of captures and shares.

### For tools and scripts

- `hocus`: agents' states (`hocus agent`), `hocus msg` to ask the
  desktop about its screens, input devices, idle state or screen reader
  tree, or to lock it, and `hocus settings [SECTION]`.
- Every setting with its default in
  [packaging/config.toml](../../packaging/config.toml), followed live as
  the file changes.

## Known limits

- The Settings' "Calendar and tasks" page is not built: the day's sources
  are chosen from the bar. CalDAV and `.ics` calendars are not read;
  only the first task board is used.
- The bar shows on the primary screen by default; `[panel] screens =
  "all"` puts the same bar on every screen.
- Screens: no mirroring, no rotation, no fractional scale chosen
  automatically (1 or 2); X11 applications look soft on a screen at
  scale 2. Night light has no sunset-to-sunrise mode.
- Screen sharing copies frames in shared memory (no DMA-BUF), asks every
  time, and serves no virtual screens; there is no Screenshot or
  RemoteDesktop portal and no screencopy protocol for other programs.
  One recording at a time.
- The screen reader does not cover the Settings window and the launcher
  yet, and does not expose a text field's caret.
- The magnifier does not follow the caret of applications without
  text-input-v3.
- Idle does not honour logind's `idle` inhibitors (`systemd-inhibit`);
  the portal's `Inhibit` (Flatpak applications) is not served.
- Notifications have no sounds, no grouping by application, and bubbles
  are not reached from the keyboard.
- The launcher converts no currencies and searches no files.
- Drawing on the screen fades away only: no lasting strokes, shapes,
  colours or undo.
- The clipboard's history does not record the primary selection.
- Not in v1: tiling; agents driving the desktop with their own pointer; a
  natural-language command; FreeBSD; plugins.
