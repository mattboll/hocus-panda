# Ideas: what people dream of

Brainstorm of 2026-10-21, beyond the [feature list](features.md). Think
Compiz, science fiction (Matrix, Minority Report, Her, Iron Man) and AI. Each
idea says why it is worth it: an idea that is only pretty must really be
pretty; a useful one must serve every day.

Tags: **V** visual, **F** functional, **AI** artificial intelligence.
Size: S (days), M (weeks), L (large).

Every kept idea must be checkable by an AI: replayable scenario, compared
screenshots, readable scene state (see the end).

## 1. The desktop as a space

1. **Infinite map desktop** (V F, L) — Windows live on a borderless plane. Zoom out to see everything, zoom into a region to work there: desktops become named places on the map that you reach with a gesture. Zoomed out, windows stay alive and readable. "Desktop 3" becomes "where project X is".
2. **Physical windows** (V F, M) — Windows have inertia: flick one towards the other screen or an edge, it glides and snaps. Windows push each other gently instead of overlapping when you drop one.
3. **Cube and wall of desktops** (V, M) — A wall of every desktop of every screen, in relief, where you grab a window and drop it elsewhere.
4. **Depth of field** (V, M) — Windows behind the active one are slightly blurred and dimmed, like a photo; the blur follows the focus. You see where you are without hiding anything.
5. **World light** (V, M) — A virtual light source that follows the time of day (morning sun from the east, orange in the evening): window shadows turn and stretch, the desktop tint follows. The desktop lives without distracting.
6. **X-ray** (V F, S) — Holding a key turns every window into glass to see beneath (an icon on the desktop, a hidden window); release makes them opaque again.

## 2. Science-fiction interfaces

7. **Mid-air gestures** (V F, L) — Minority Report: the webcam follows the hand (local model); close your fist to grab a window, throw it to the other screen, swipe to switch desktops. First for presenting and for the "wow", opt-in.
8. **The screen you look at** (F, M) — The webcam only guesses which screen you are looking at. The pointer jumps there, shortcuts apply there, notifications show there. With two screens, no more mouse round trips.
9. **Radial menu at the pointer** (V F, S) — Super+click opens a circular menu around the pointer: close, minimize, maximize, snap, send to the other screen, pin. Replaces the title bar without losing its actions; learnt by gesture (up = maximize…).
10. **HUD** (V, M) — A head-up theme: thin glowing lines, scans, chromatic aberration during transitions, discreet system information on the edges. Matrix and Tron as full themes, not just wallpapers.
11. **Glowing edges** (V F, S) — Screen edges pulse in the colour of the app that notifies (Slack purple…), like phones: you see who calls without a bubble over your work.
12. **Living wallpaper** (V, M) — Parallax in relief with the pointer, Matrix rain that speeds up when you type, waves with sound, colour from the weather or the machine load.

## 3. Smarter windows

13. **Tabs across applications** (F, M) — Drop a window on another to group them in tabs, whatever the apps. The group moves, snaps and hides as one.
14. **Live window clip** (F, M) — Cut an area of any window (a dashboard chart, the end of a log, a meeting video) into a small live floating window, always on top. Picture in picture, for everything.
15. **Contexts** (F, M) — A context = apps, windows, places on each screen, desktops, do not disturb. "Client X", "Meeting", "Personal": switch at once, everything moves; come back, everything is as before. The AI suggests contexts from what you open together.
16. **Undo for windows** (F, S) — Desktop Ctrl+Z: reopen the window closed by mistake (at least relaunch it in place), undo a tidy-up, a move to another screen.
17. **Summon a window** (F, S) — Typing an app name brings its window to the current screen and desktop instead of going there.
18. **Sound per window** (F, S) — A small wave on the window that plays sound; click to mute or adjust. No more hunting the noisy tab.
19. **Keyboard hint mode** (F AI, M) — Like Vimium for the whole desktop: one key, every visible clickable element gets letters (from the accessibility tree), type to click. Serves keyboard users, accessibility, and AI agents (same labels).

## 4. Memory and search

20. **Rewind** (F AI, L) — A local, encrypted, light recording of what was on screen, with recognised text. A timeline to see the screen ten minutes ago; a search for "the ticket with the red chart I opened yesterday". Strict exclusions: password fields, private windows, chosen apps; pause with a gesture.
21. **Circle to ask** (F AI, S) — Draw a circle around anything on screen: search, translate, copy the text, explain the error, add to a note. The annotation pen already exists; it becomes a question.
22. **Overlay translation** (F AI, M) — Translate a window or an area in place, as an overlay that follows scrolling.

## 5. AI in the desktop

23. **Agent seat** (AI, L) — An agent (Claude Code…) has its own pointer and keyboard, distinct from yours: it clicks and types in its windows while you work in yours, without stealing focus. Its cursor is visible, with its name and colour.
24. **Agent desktop** (AI, M) — A virtual screen where agents work, shown as a live thumbnail; enlarge to watch, take over with a click, hand back.
25. **Agent API** (AI, M) — What the agent sees and does, over MCP: tree of windows and their content (accessibility), window capture, clicks, typing, scrolling aimed at a window rather than coordinates, waiting for a state ("window X shows Y"). This is what makes the agent reliable instead of guessing on pixels.
26. **Permissions and journal** (AI, M) — Each agent has rights (which apps, which folders, read or act); every action is logged and replayable as video; one key freezes every agent at once.
27. **The agent shows before acting** (AI V, S) — Before a click, a target appears on the element with the intent in one line ("I'm sending the message"); sensitive actions are confirmed with a gesture.
28. **Learn by showing** (AI, L) — Do a task once while recording; the AI derives a reusable, generalised automation ("do the same for the 30 invoices").
29. **Notification triage** (AI, M) — The AI groups and summarises notifications, decides what may interrupt while you focus, proposes replies (Slack) sent in one click.
30. **Voice** (AI F, M) — Push to talk to the command field (local recognition), and dictation in any app.
31. **Meeting copilot** (AI, M) — Local transcription of the meeting, summary and action items in the notes, a reminder of what was said when you come back.
32. **First-class agents** (AI, M) — Generalise the agents widget: every agent (Claude Code in an agent manager such as herdr or on its own, others) with its state, what it waits for, and replies from the notification or the command field.

## 6. Privacy and screen sharing

33. **Safe screen sharing** (F, S) — While sharing, windows marked sensitive (chat, mail, password manager) are blurred automatically, and notifications held.
34. **Camera, microphone, capture indicators** (F, S) — A coloured dot when the camera, microphone or a capture is active, with which app; one gesture cuts everything.
35. **Virtual screen to share** (F, M) — Share a screen made only of chosen windows, wherever they really are.
36. **Two pointers** (F, L) — Share a window with a colleague who can point and click in it with their own cursor (pair programming).

## 7. Presenting

37. **Presentation mode** (V F, S) — Spotlight on the pointer, magnifier, keys shown, laser, pen, notifications muted, separate speaker screen.

## 8. Phone

38. **Continuity** (F, M) — Phone notifications and SMS on the desktop, reply from the bubble; the phone as a webcam; drop a file on the phone icon (KDE Connect and adb already there).

## First triage (2026-10-21)

- **Kept**: AI in the desktop (23 to 25, 27 to 32); circle to ask (21).
- **In doubt, maybe too heavy**: replayable journal and freeze key (26); rewind (20).
- **To push until it shows real value, could be "wow"**: the screen you look at (8), which feels heavy and intrusive.
- **Not visualised**: infinite map (1); showing the whole desktop is rarely needed.
- **To judge on mockups**: Compiz-like effects (2 to 6, 10 to 12); productivity (13 to 19). Mockups exist for all of them.
- **Not discussed yet**: mid-air gestures (7), radial menu (9, mocked), privacy (33 to 36), presenting (37), phone (38).

## AI-testable from the design

Every kept feature comes with:
- a **scenario** replayable without a screen (actions, expectations), written before the code;
- a **readable state**: the scene (windows, positions, running effects) can be queried, no guessing from an image;
- **reference screenshots** compared on every pull request, with a tolerance for effects;
- for webcam or microphone ideas, a **simulated input** (recorded images or sounds) so the scenario runs without hardware.

The agent API (idea 25) serves both: it lets Claude Code use the desktop, and test it.
